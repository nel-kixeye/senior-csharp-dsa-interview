Attempts:
Attempt 1 - WRONG (CS0029: cannot convert 'void' to 'int[]').
  Assumed IntersectWith RETURNED a new set. It MUTATES the receiver and
  returns void. Lesson: check the return type of set operations.

Attempt 2 - WRONG (CS0030: cannot convert HashSet<int> to int[]).
  Tried to CAST: (int[])setA.
  Lesson: cast vs convert. A cast reinterprets something that already IS a T.
  HashSet<int> and int[] are unrelated types - you need a CONVERSION that
  builds a new object (ToArray, or the [.. set] collection expression).

Attempt 3 - CORRECT but two sets. 9/9 pass.

Attempt 4 - refinement to ONE set.
  IntersectWith takes IEnumerable<int>, so it consumes `b` directly.
  The second HashSet was pure overhead.

Attempt 5 - WRONG (3 of 9 failed). Tried to hit O(min(n,m)) by picking the
  smaller array, using TWO independent ternaries:
      smaller = a.Length <  b.Length ? a : b;
      larger  = a.Length >  b.Length ? a : b;
  When the lengths are EQUAL both conditions are false, so BOTH variables
  became b - it intersected b with itself.
  Every failing case had a.Length == b.Length. The "identical arrays" test
  still PASSED by luck, which hid the bug.

Attempt 6 - CORRECT. Changed > to >= so the two conditions are exhaustive.
  13/13 pass including equal-length variants, both-empty, and symmetry.
  O(n + m) time, O(min(n, m)) space - target met.

THE LESSON WORTH KEEPING (bigger than this problem):
  Two independent ternaries over the same comparison do NOT partition the
  input. `<` and `>` both fall through on `==`. When splitting two ways, make
  the second condition the NEGATION of the first, not a separate test.
